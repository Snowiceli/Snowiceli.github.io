## Current

### University of Wisconsin–Madison
*Madison, Wisconsin, United States*  
**PhD Student in Botany | 2026–present**  
Advisor: Prof. Daijiang Li

My research examines how urbanization and environmental change shape plant communities and species interactions, connecting my background in landscape design and plant physiology with community ecology and biogeography.

**Urbanization and global patterns of plant diversity**

I investigate how urbanization reshapes plant community composition and biotic homogenization across cities worldwide. My current work examines the roles of native and non-native species, latitudinal variation, and differences between urban cores and surrounding landscapes. I am particularly interested in how the choice of comparison baseline changes our understanding of urban homogenization.

**Climate change, urban plants, and species interactions**

I am developing a research focus on how climate change and extreme weather affect urban plant communities and species interactions, particularly plant–pollinator relationships. I am interested in how urban environments modify plant responses, flowering phenology, and interactions with pollinators.
